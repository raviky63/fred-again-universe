## Anecdotes
- NAME: the "again.." comes from the 2002 live-action Scooby-Doo film, the body-swap scene where Daphne, in Fred's body, says "I'm Fred again!". Fred confirmed it by nodding along in a 2023 TikTok stitch. (Yahoo News / TV Tropes)
- NAPLES ROOFTOP (Sept 2025): after his open-air show in Naples' city centre he went up to the roof of a fan, Fede, overlooking the piazza and played an hour-long set for Fede and an older woman who lives in the same building. He finds it hard to wind down after shows, so he played songs he was working on while the crowd drifted home. Posted to YouTube as "Rooftop Live (Fede's Roof, Naples)". (MusicRadar)
- LA COLISEUM (14 June 2024), first stadium show: idea came during the Shrine residency the year before. Fred played from six spots and walked a moat around the field; Obongjayar sang from the stands among fans; Romy and Fred started "Strong" facing each other; an MPC battle with Tony on opposite sides of the moat. (Rolling Stone)
- SHRINE RESIDENCY LA (Oct–Nov 2023): night six fell on Halloween; crowd in costumes, a huge LED "video diary" as the visual centrepiece. (UCLA Radio)
- BELGIUM 2023: the crowd jumped so hard that his controller stand shook and a crew member had to hold the gear while he played. Exact show not named. (George FM)
- BANGKOK 2025: Fred did a lap through the crowd during "Victory Lap Four". (Rolling Stone India)
- NEW ZEALAND: ran to a second stage in the middle of the arena, pulled fans up, and brought Wellington DJ MESSIE (Tessa Hills) on stage. (NZ Herald)
- ROMY on Strong: nervous at first, reassured by Fred; called playing in the round "a very, very dreamlike scenario". (Rolling Stone)
- Delilah (pull me out of this) is built on Delilah Montagu's vocal "Lost Keys" (substack essay — weak source, verify)

## Crew in their own words
- LUCY HICKLING / LOOSE (creative director): ~25 at time of the interview; Fred calls her "an absolutely mental human being" and says she thinks like a very creative child; LA one week, Sydney the next; tries bold ideas, treats failures as lessons; doesn't see herself as an artist, prefers making things for others. (DB Journey "Pack Heavy, Chase Light")
  On LA Coliseum: helped plan the moat so every artist could reach the whole crowd; wanted to keep the intimacy; a second stage mirrored the moat for the more complex songs; worked with design team Human Person. (Rolling Stone)
- THEO BATTERHAM (photographer): ~5 years documenting Fred; photography started when his older brother's school project turned their bathroom into a darkroom; shoots from behind the decks and deep in the crowd; cares more about the feeling around a moment than the final image; aims for both the peak and the comedown. Covered the six-night NYC USB002 residency. (Milk Studios / The Agents Club)
- JAMIE TINSLEY (sound): chose a roofless stadium because roofs bounce the cheers back; added 21 cardioid speaker stacks halfway up the stands and a ring of 68 triple-21" cardioid subs around the stage; goal: nobody further than 60 m from a speaker. (Rolling Stone)
- JENN YACOUBIAN (Goldenvoice EVP): never saw an event so intentional about sound. (Rolling Stone)
- EXPLORERS CLUB / AARON SKIPPER (USB002 identity): one stark black-and-grey flag as the symbol; hand-cut typeface, lo-fi analog craft; 2:1 flag system so each of the 10 cities got its own version (flag wrapping the Glasgow venue, screen version in Madrid, cathedral facade in Lyon); 60-foot flag unfurled from a six-storey building for the launch video; fans photographed and remixed their city's flag; Boris Acket's ceiling installation moved the flag above the crowd with the music. (Hypebot)
- BORIS ACKET: kinetic fabric-and-gravity installation "Einder" on the USB002 tour. (Hypebot; substack)
- RUBBERBAND. (Jason Sondock & Simon Davis): trilogy = HARDSTYLE 2, scared, Lights Burn Dimmer; black and white, protagonist "Brandon"; each scene 10–13 seconds of real time shot at 500 fps in single takes, stretched to ~3.5 minutes; concept from talks with Fred and LOOSE; references Duane Michals' Sequences, Goya's Black Paintings, the play "I'm Looking For Helen Twelvetrees". (Hero Magazine)
